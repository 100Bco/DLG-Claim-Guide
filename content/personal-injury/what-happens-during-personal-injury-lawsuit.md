A personal injury lawsuit formally begins when a plaintiff files a complaint against a defendant in civil court. This document outlines the legal basis for the claim, alleging that the defendant’s negligence or intentional act caused harm to the plaintiff, and demands compensation for resulting damages.

Once the complaint is filed and the defendant is served, they have a limited time to file an answer. This initial exchange of documents is known as the pleadings phase. It establishes the basic arguments and defenses that will frame the rest of the litigation.

### The Discovery Phase

After the pleadings are complete, the case enters discovery. This is often the longest phase of a lawsuit. During discovery, both sides exchange relevant information and evidence. This typically involves:

*   **Interrogatories:** Written questions that the other party must answer under oath.
*   **Requests for Production:** Demands for physical documents, digital records, medical files, or other tangible evidence.
*   **Depositions:** In-person or remote interviews where witnesses or parties to the lawsuit answer questions from opposing counsel under oath.

The purpose of discovery is to eliminate surprises at trial. By the end of this phase, both sides should have a clear understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of the opposing case.

### Negotiation and Settlement

Because civil trials are expensive, unpredictable, and public, the vast majority of personal injury lawsuits do not go to trial. Instead, they are resolved through settlement.

Settlement negotiations can happen at any time—even before a lawsuit is formally filed. However, they often accelerate toward the end of the discovery phase, once both sides have evaluated the evidence. Courts frequently mandate alternative dispute resolution, such as mediation, where a neutral third party helps facilitate a settlement agreement between the plaintiff and the defendant.

If a settlement is reached, the plaintiff agrees to dismiss the lawsuit and sign a release of liability in exchange for a negotiated sum of money. If no agreement can be reached, the case proceeds to trial, where a judge or jury will hear the evidence and render a binding verdict.
